Live-M.

A lightweight terminal system monitor that replaces htop and psensors — and never touches the process list.

Per-core CPU, memory, swap, disks, temperatures and live Wi-Fi throughput in one calm, colour-coded view. Everything updates in place, so you can leave it open on a second monitor and forget about it.

What it shows

Everything that matters.
Nothing that doesn't.

Live-M is deliberately not a process explorer. It answers one question well: how is this machine doing right now?

Per-core CPU

Colour-coded usage bars for every core, two columns on wide terminals, plus the overall average and 1/5/15-minute load.

RAM and swap

Used and total memory in GiB with a clear percentage bar for each, so pressure is obvious at a glance.

Real disks only

Every real mountpoint gets a bar. tmpfs, snap mounts and /boot are filtered out instead of cluttering the panel.

Temperatures

CPU package on a full-width bar, per-core temperatures underneath, and any other detected sensor that reports useful data.

Live Wi-Fi speed

Current download and upload throughput sit in the header, next to the hostname, without taking over the screen.

Lighter than htop

Live-M skips /proc/[pid] entirely. CPU usage stays under 0.5%, which is the whole reason it exists.

Why this exists

Small on purpose.

htop walks every process in /proc/[pid]/ on every refresh. That is useful when you need a process list — and unnecessary weight when you only want the health of the machine.

Live-M drops the process iteration completely. The result is a monitor that stays under 0.5% CPU, starts instantly and looks good doing it.

  • Live-updating panels, no flicker
  • Refresh interval adjustable with + and -
  • Optional --interval, --no-color, --version and --help
  • Quit with q or Ctrl+C
